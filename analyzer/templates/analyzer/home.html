1

Look it up

If the headline is one already recorded in our fact-check database, you get that record — a lookup, not a guess. This is the most reliable answer the tool gives.

2

Read the writing

Otherwise a language model trained on tens of thousands of real and fake articles reads the text itself and says which it looks more like. It tells you how often verdicts like yours turn out correct.

3

Ask the web

When the model lands in the range where it is measurably unreliable, we search the news for the story and show what we find — including who is debunking it.

This is a student project, not a fact-checker. Every verdict is a prediction with a real error rate, and it is shown to you alongside the verdict for exactly that reason. Check anything that matters against several trusted outlets.
